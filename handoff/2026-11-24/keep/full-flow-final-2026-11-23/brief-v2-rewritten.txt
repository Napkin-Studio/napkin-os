TIDEWELL SPARKLING WATER · V1 · 23 NOV 2026
Tide's In
CLIENT
Tidewell Sparkling Water, launching Tidewell Sea Mineral Sparkling: a lime and cucumber sparkling water with a pinch of sea minerals, in a slim 330 ml can.
PROJECT
Tide's In
BUDGET & SCOPE
Budget: £150,000 including VAT, with media outside it and paid by the client (agreed since the client's email, which said only 'smaller than we'd like, TBC'). Scope: April teaser, May launch, posters near stations and festival sites, Instagram and TikTok, a 15-second film that also runs as pre-roll, sampling at running events, possibly a radio spot, and one shareable mechanic. No Christmas phase and no still range work. Ideas due next Wednesday.
THE JOB
To confirm: Move young professionals from seeing a soft drink in the round as the one they must explain to seeing Tidewell as the drink they are happy to be seen holding.
WHERE WE ARE
Tidewell Sea Mineral Sparkling launches in May, with April noise, into a water category led by San Pellegrino (26.4% consideration) and Highland Spring (23.8%). 20% of Gen Z adults in the UK do not drink alcohol, yet a third of UK consumers feel uncomfortable ordering alcohol-free socially. Pubs are both customer and stage. Christmas and the still range are out of scope.
THE BARRIER
A soft drink at the round feels dull and invites questions. Beer is the default, and another sparkling water gives no reason to switch. A third of UK consumers report discomfort ordering alcohol-free products in social settings, so the fear of judgement is felt even where moderation is accepted.

OBJECTIVE · COMMERCIAL
In the six months after the May launch: 250,000 cans sampled and 800 pub and bar listings. Retail sales are read through sales data; no sales figure has been given.
OBJECTIVE · BEHAVIOURAL
After-work young professionals choose Tidewell in the round instead of the beer or the sugary drink, and try it through sampling and in pubs. Sharing, such as a challenge or a filter, is secondary to this.
OBJECTIVE · ATTITUDINAL
Tidewell is seen as a cool, grown-up drink with character: the one you are happy to hold at a festival and the sensible one in the office fridge, never a consolation prize and never pious. The still range benefits by association only.
WHO WE ARE TALKING TO
Lead: urban 25-40 after-work young professionals who are cutting back on alcohol or sugar, including the sober curious and those saving money where a round costs a small fortune. They stay in the round and want something decent in hand. Festival-goers and runners are supporting audiences. Parents and 'everyone who drinks water' are not the lead.
COMPETITOR CONTEXT
San Pellegrino and Highland Spring lead water consideration (26.4% and 23.8%). Highland Spring owns purity and origin ('Water as nature intended'). Perrier was advertised as the 'Champagne of mineral water'. Alcohol-free beer sits in a masculine beer frame: participants called Heineken 0.0 'a bloke drink'. Hard seltzers claim a clean-living image. The open space is a mineral, coastal adult fizz that is neither wellness-pious nor beer mimicry. The client also names flavoured seltzers and its own still range.
CONSUMER INSIGHT
I want to stay in the round without being the one who has to explain why I'm not drinking.
SINGLE-MINDED PROPOSITION
To confirm: Tidewell is the sea-fresh fizz grown-up enough to be seen with at any round.

THE OBJECTION TO BEAT
To confirm: "It's just water with bubbles."
REASONS TO BELIEVE
A pinch of sea minerals in every can: the product's own difference from plain sparkling water.
Zero sugar and zero calories, which the client has cleared. Any wording must meet the GB register's conditions of use.
To confirm: recyclable can and 1% for the oceans, once the client's proof is held and the claims are substantiated and qualified under CAP Rule 11.4.
To confirm: minerals from the Cornish coast, only once legal has cleared 'Cornish'.
RESPONSE · THINK
To confirm: Tidewell is a proper adult drink with real character, not water with bubbles.
RESPONSE · FEEL
To confirm: Fresh, confident and included, with no sense of missing out.
RESPONSE · DO
To confirm: Pick up or ask for Tidewell at the next after-work round, and try it at a sampling event.
TONE & WORLD
Fresh but grown up
Fun but not silly
Coastal but not cliché: no shells, anchors or puns
Wry and never preachy or wellness-pious
Clearly not like the big brands

Station posters: catch the after-work commute.
Running-event sampling: first taste for the 250,000-can goal.
15-second film: launch fame, also as pre-roll.

MANDATORIES
The can is the hero, with the logo bottom left.
No real, recognisable faces (lookalike issue last year).
Never show the product with alcohol in shot.
Include: "Contains naturally occurring sea minerals. Enjoy as part of a balanced diet."
Use 'Cornish' only once legal has cleared it.
Health and environmental wording must meet the CAP Code, the GB register and substantiation rules.
OPEN QUESTIONS
What is the budget, does it include VAT, and does media sit inside it? — Answer: £150,000 including VAT; media buying sits outside it and is paid by the client.
What are the sales, distribution or trial targets, and over what period? — Answer: Retail sales and trial: 250,000 cans sampled and 800 pub and bar listings in the first six months after the May launch.
You also sell to pubs: how do you want to handle a message about swapping out beer? — Answer: Same message in pubs: Tidewell is the one you have in the round, never an attack on beer.
If we must choose, which audience leads: after-work young professionals, festival-goers or runners? — Answer: After-work young professionals aged 25 to 40 lead; festival-goers and runners are supporting audiences.
Has legal cleared zero sugar, zero calories and 'Cornish', and what proof do you hold for the 1% for the oceans pledge? — Answer: Zero sugar and zero calories are cleared. Cornish is not cleared yet, so use minerals from the coast. We hold the 1% for the Planet membership certificate.
Is the Christmas phase in this brief, and does the still range need its own message? — Answer: No. May launch with April teaser only; no Christmas phase, and the still range stays out.
How will you judge success, and can you run a before-and-after tracker or a holdout? — Answer: Retail sales data plus a before-and-after tracker wave; no holdout.
