Tide's In
CLIENT
Tidewell Sparkling Water, launching Tidewell Sea Mineral Sparkling, a lime and cucumber sparkling water with a pinch of sea minerals in a slim 330 ml can.
PROJECT
Tide's In
BUDGET & SCOPE
The client says budget is smaller than they would like and TBC; no figure has been given. Confirm the figure, whether it includes VAT and whether media sits inside it. Scope: April noise, May launch, posters near stations and festival sites, Instagram and TikTok, a 15-second film that also runs as pre-roll, sampling at running events, possibly a radio spot, and one shareable mechanic. Christmas and the still range are out of this brief. Ideas are due next Wednesday.
THE JOB
To confirm: Move young professionals from seeing a soft drink at the round as the explain-yourself choice to ordering Tidewell as a drink they're happy to be seen with.
WHERE WE ARE
Tidewell Sea Mineral Sparkling launches in May, with noise in April. It enters a water category where San Pellegrino leads consideration (26.4%) and Highland Spring follows (23.8%). Moderation is growing among the young: 20% of Gen Z adults in the UK do not drink alcohol. Pubs are both a customer and a stage for the brand. The still range and Christmas are outside this brief.
THE BARRIER
A soft drink at the round feels dull and in need of explanation. Beer is the default, and another sparkling water gives no reason to switch. A third of UK consumers report discomfort ordering alcohol-free products in social settings, so the fear of being questioned is real even where moderation is accepted.
PAGE 2 OF 5
OBJECTIVE · COMMERCIAL
In the six months after the May launch: 250,000 cans sampled and 800 pub and bar listings. Retail sales are read through sales data; no sales figure has been given.
OBJECTIVE · BEHAVIOURAL
Young professionals choose Tidewell at the after-work round in place of the beer or the sugary drink, and try it through sampling. Sharing, whether a challenge or a filter, is secondary to this.
OBJECTIVE · ATTITUDINAL
Tidewell is seen as a cool, grown-up choice, the one you are happy to hold at a festival and the sensible one in the office fridge, never a consolation prize. The still range benefits by association only.
WHO WE ARE TALKING TO
Lead: 25-40 urban young professionals who go out after work and are cutting back on alcohol or sugar, including the sober curious. Some are also saving money where a round costs a small fortune. Festival-goers and runners are supporting audiences. Parents and 'everyone who drinks water' are not the lead.
COMPETITOR CONTEXT
San Pellegrino leads water consideration at 26.4%. Highland Spring leads on purity and origin ('Water As Nature Intended'). Perrier was advertised as the 'Champagne of mineral water'. Alcohol-free beer stays in a beer frame: participants called Heineken 0.0 'a bloke drink'. Hard seltzers lean on a clean-living image. The open space is a mineral, coastal, adult fizz that is neither wellness-pious nor beer mimicry.
CONSUMER INSIGHT
To confirm: I want to stay in the round without being the one who has to explain why I'm not drinking.
SINGLE-MINDED PROPOSITION
To confirm: Tidewell is the sea-fresh fizz grown-up enough to be seen with at any round.

THE OBJECTION TO BEAT
To confirm: "It's just water with bubbles."
REASONS TO BELIEVE
A pinch of sea minerals in every can: the product's own difference from plain sparkling water, and the answer to 'just water with bubbles'.
Zero sugar and zero calories. To confirm: the client's check and legal sign-off, with wording that meets the GB register's conditions of use.
To confirm: recyclable can and 1% for the oceans, once the client's proof is held and the claims are substantiated and qualified under CAP Rule 11.4.
RESPONSE · THINK
To confirm: Tidewell is a proper adult drink with real character, not water with bubbles.
RESPONSE · FEEL
To confirm: Fresh, confident and included, with no sense of missing out.
RESPONSE · DO
To confirm: Pick up a Tidewell at the next after-work round, ask for it at the pub, and try it at a sampling event.
TONE & WORLD
Fresh but grown up
Fun but not silly
Coastal but not cliché: no shells, anchors or seaside puns
Wry and never preachy or wellness-pious
Clearly not like the big brands
ROLE OF EACH MEDIUM
To confirm: Pubs and bars: put Tidewell in the round, where the choice is made and listings are won.
Station posters: catch the after-work commute.
Running-event sampling: first taste, which the 250,000-can goal needs.
15-second film: launch fame, also as pre-roll.

MANDATORIES
The can is the hero, logo bottom left.
No real, recognisable faces (lookalike issue last year).
Never show the product with alcohol in shot.
Include: 'Contains naturally occurring sea minerals. Enjoy as part of a balanced diet.'
Use 'Cornish' only once legal has cleared it.
Health and environmental wording must fit the CAP Code, the GB register and substantiation rules.
OPEN QUESTIONS
What is the budget, does it include VAT, and does media sit inside it? — Answer: £150,000 including VAT; media buying sits outside it and is paid by the client.
What are the sales, distribution or trial targets, and over what period? — Answer: Retail sales and trial: 250,000 cans sampled and 800 pub and bar listings in the first six months after the May launch.
You also sell to pubs: how do you want to handle a message about swapping out beer? — Answer: Same message in pubs: Tidewell is the one you have in the round, never an attack on beer.
If we must choose, which audience leads: after-work young professionals, festival-goers or runners? — Answer: After-work young professionals aged 25 to 40 lead; festival-goers and runners are supporting audiences.
Has legal cleared zero sugar, zero calories and 'Cornish', and what proof do you hold for the 1% for the oceans pledge? — Answer: Zero sugar and zero calories are cleared. Cornish is not cleared yet, so use minerals from the coast. We hold the 1% for the Planet membership certificate.
Is the Christmas phase in this brief, and does the still range need its own message? — Answer: No. May launch with April teaser only; no Christmas phase, and the still range stays out.
How will you judge success, and can you run a before-and-after tracker or a holdout? — Answer: Retail sales data plus a before-and-after tracker wave; no holdout.
