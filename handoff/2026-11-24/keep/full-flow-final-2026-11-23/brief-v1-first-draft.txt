BUDGET & SCOPE
Deliverables: posters near stations and festival sites; Instagram and TikTok; a 15-second film that can also run as YouTube pre-roll; sampling at running events; possibly a radio spot. Timing: April noise, May launch, a possible Christmas party season phase; ideas due next Wednesday. Budget: smaller than the client would like and not yet given. Confirm whether it includes VAT and whether media sits inside it.
THE JOB
To confirm: Get a young professional to pick a Tidewell at the after-work round instead of a sugary can or a second beer.
WHERE WE ARE
Tidewell Sea Mineral Sparkling is a new lime and cucumber water with a pinch of sea minerals, in a slim 330 ml can. It launches in May, with noise wanted in April. The shelf is led by San Pellegrino (26.4% consideration) and Highland Spring (23.8%); Perrier has 6.5%. Not drinking is mainstream among the young: 20% of Gen Z adults in the UK do not drink alcohol.
THE BARRIER
Water at a social occasion can feel like the dull, second-best choice, a sign you are opting out. Sugary drinks feel like a guilty treat and beer is the default round. Young drinkers also worry about the emotional impact of alcohol, yet still have to handle social drinking norms. Another sparkling water gives them no reason to switch.

OBJECTIVE · COMMERCIAL
Establish distribution and sales for the new can, and lift the still range by association. Targets, timeframe and any pub listing goals have not been given. To confirm with the client.
OBJECTIVE · BEHAVIOURAL
Get young professionals to choose Tidewell at the after-work round in place of the sugary drink or the beer. Sharing the can on social is a secondary behaviour.
OBJECTIVE · ATTITUDINAL
Tidewell is seen as a cool, grown-up choice: the one you are happy to hold at a festival and the sensible one in the office fridge, never a consolation prize.
WHO WE ARE TALKING TO
Lead with 25-40 urban young professionals who go out after work, go to festivals and are cutting back on drinking or sugar: the sober curious and moderators. Some are saving money in cities where a round costs a small fortune. Runners are a sampling cohort. Parents and 'everyone who drinks water' are not the lead, because a message for everyone lands with no one.
COMPETITOR CONTEXT
Highland Spring sells nature and simplicity ('Water as nature intended'). San Pellegrino leads consideration at 26.4%; Perrier has 6.5%. Alcohol-free beer is stuck in a beer frame: Heineken 0.0 was called 'a bloke drink'. The open space is a mineral, coastal, adult fizz that is neither wellness-pious nor a beer substitute.
CONSUMER INSIGHT
To confirm: I want to stay in the round without being the one who has to explain why I'm drinking a soft drink.
SINGLE-MINDED PROPOSITION
Tidewell is the sea-fresh fizz that is grown-up enough to be seen with at any round.

THE OBJECTION TO BEAT
To confirm: "It's just water."
REASONS TO BELIEVE
A pinch of sea minerals in every can.
Zero sugar, zero calories: awaiting evidence. Any 'no added sugars' wording must meet the conditions of use, as the ASA's Trip ruling shows.
Minerals from the Cornish coast: awaiting evidence, as legal is checking the word 'Cornish'.
1% for the oceans: awaiting evidence, as proof of the pledge is needed.
RESPONSE · THINK
To confirm: Tidewell is a proper adult drink with real character, not just water with bubbles.
RESPONSE · FEEL
To confirm: Fresh, confident and included, with no sense of missing out.
RESPONSE · DO
To confirm: Pick up a Tidewell at the next after-work round.
TONE & WORLD
Adult, wry and fresh, like a good bar's salt-air terrace rather than a beach postcard
Confident and a little dry; fun without being silly
Never preachy, wellness-pious or full of seaside puns and shells
ROLE OF EACH MEDIUM

Attribution: a geo holdout comparing sales in exposed and unexposed areas.

MANDATORIES
The can is the hero, with the logo bottom left.
No real, recognisable faces.
Never show the product with alcohol in shot.
Include: 'Contains naturally occurring sea minerals. Enjoy as part of a balanced diet.'
Legal to clear 'Cornish', zero sugar and zero calories; health-type wording must follow the CAP Code and the GB register.
