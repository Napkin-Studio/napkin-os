Hollow Oak 'Still'
CLIENT
Hollow Oak Cider, a 140-year-old, orchard-grown brand with a loyal 55+ rural core, launching its first still cider.
PROJECT
Hollow Oak 'Still'
BUDGET & SCOPE
Budget is small: what is left over from last year's Christmas production, to be known after Q1. No figure was given, so we need the figure, whether it includes VAT and whether media sits inside it. Prioritise the 15-second film for social, which could also serve TV if affordable, plus stills. Three routes by Friday; the board wants to see work by month end. Summer builds anticipation; September drives trial.
THE JOB
To confirm: move a 25-40 drinker who rarely has cider from 'cider is fizzy and not for me' to 'a still orchard cider is worth trying'.
WHERE WE ARE
Still launches in September and nothing is on shelf before then, so summer work builds anticipation, not sales. Premium is where cider is growing: GB crafted cider value is up 7.6% while retail volume is down 1.7%, and shoppers are trading up to a single good bottle as a treat.
THE BARRIER
To confirm: 'Cider without bubbles sounds flat.' To a rare cider drinker, no fizz reads as a cider that has lost something, and cider already feels sweet and heavy.
PAGE 2 OF 5
OBJECTIVE · COMMERCIAL
Grow total Hollow Oak sales by 30%, all products included, not just Still. The client gave no baseline or period. We propose 12 months from the September launch against the prior year, for the client to confirm. Winning an award is an ambition, not a business objective.
OBJECTIVE · BEHAVIOURAL
To confirm: Get 25-40 drinkers who rarely drink cider to look for Still and try it from September. Core drinkers keep buying the current bottle.
OBJECTIVE · ATTITUDINAL
To confirm: shift the lead drinker from 'cider is not for me' to 'a still orchard cider is worth trying', and build awareness of Still among them. Making Hollow Oak cool with Gen Z is secondary, and the 55+ rural core must not be alienated.
WHO WE ARE TALKING TO
To confirm: lead with 25-40 drinkers who rarely drink cider, women slightly ahead. At a friend's table, in the pub or at a Friday supper they reach for lager, wine or kombucha. They like to treat themselves to one good bottle rather than a bulk pack. The brief is not led by students, pub landlords or Gen Z. The 55+ rural core is protected, not targeted.
COMPETITOR CONTEXT
The client names big lager and kombucha, which stand for easy refreshment. Among ciders, Magners owns fizz and serving over ice. Strongbow is moving from orchard and rural codes to everyday British culture. No checked rival claims a still orchard position, so Still may be able to own it.
CONSUMER INSIGHT
To confirm: "I like the idea of an honest apple drink, but cider has come to mean fizzy, sweet and heavy, so I order something else."
SINGLE-MINDED PROPOSITION
To confirm: Hollow Oak Still gives you the whole orchard in every glass, with nothing fizzy or heavy in the way.

MANDATORIES
Legal: responsible drinking line.
Legal: no reduced-sugar, health or mood claims (CAP/BCAP); the sugar story is held until R&D confirms, and GB rules do not permit such a claim in any case.
Legal: competition needs legal terms and a no-purchase route.
To confirm: why legal wants no apples in the visuals; until explained, show none.
Brand assets: logo, the orchard and the new recyclable can.
To confirm: whether the old bottle sits beside the can.
Open items: heritage, hurling and the 1884 portrait go in other assets.
OPEN QUESTIONS
What is the production budget figure, does it include VAT, and does media sit inside it? — Answer: Production budget is 120,000 pounds all-in, excluding VAT, and media sits outside it. Spend it on the 15-second film, stills and in-store; no TV or stunt.
What is the baseline and period for the 30% sales uplift, and does it include Still? — Answer: Baseline is last year's total Hollow Oak sales volume; target is plus 30 percent over 12 months from the September launch, across all products including Still.
Can legal explain the 'no apples' concern? — Answer: Legal worry is apple imagery implying a fruit or health claim we cannot back. Hard rule: no apples anywhere in visuals. Show the oak, bare orchard trees, the can, the glass.
How is Still made without fizz, and what does that do to the taste? — Answer: Still is made like the sparkling cider but left uncarbonated, which gives a rounder, softer, drier taste. Say that plainly, with no reduced-sugar claim.
Do you accept one lead audience (25-40 rare cider drinkers) and one lead line, rather than all four 'Still' lines? — Answer: Yes. One lead audience, 25-40 drinkers who rarely drink cider, and one lead line only: Still. Hollow Oak.
What is the competition mechanic, and will legal supply the terms and a no-purchase route? — Answer: Win a weekend in the orchards. Entry on social, after launch, with a no-purchase route. Legal terms come from the client's legal team before anything is published.
Must the old bottle appear beside the can? — Answer: Yes. Show the new can as the hero and the old bottle once, small, beside it, so core drinkers recognise the brand.
Which measures count as success, and is there budget for a tracker or holdout? — Answer: Primary: trial of Still among 25-40s, read through sales and distribution after September. Secondary: awareness in a before-and-after tracker. An award is a bonus.
