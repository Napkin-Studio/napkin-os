Proper Crunch
CLIENT
Harrowgate Bakery Co., launching Harrowgate Oven Crisps: baked, not fried, sourdough crisps in a 120 g bag.
PROJECT
Proper Crunch
BUDGET & SCOPE
£180,000 ex VAT, media included: one national poster burst near stations and supermarkets, Instagram and TikTok content, one 15-second film for YouTube and cinema, and sampling in about 200 stores. On shelf in March, buzz before Easter, ideas due Thursday. The pub and bread are out of scope.
THE JOB
To confirm: A meal-deal shopper stops grabbing the familiar fried crisp and picks Harrowgate Oven Crisps, because they now believe a baked crisp can be properly crunchy.
WHERE WE ARE
A bread maker launches baked sourdough crisps (120 g, sea salt & cider vinegar first) on shelf in March, wanting buzz before Easter on 5 April, so the window is short. Two meal-deal listings are confirmed and a third is in talks. Price promotion drives brand choice for 43% of buyers and only 11% buy a 'less guilty' option, so the meal deal is the route to trial.
THE BARRIER
In the seconds of a meal-deal choice, shoppers grab the crisp they know. They suspect baked means worthy and less satisfying, and an unknown bakery brand has no crisp credentials. Guilt is felt but rarely acted on (only 11% buy a 'less guilty' option), so 'healthier' alone will not move them.

OBJECTIVE · COMMERCIAL
Be listed in 3 of the big 5 supermarket meal deals by March and sell 2 million bags in the first 12 weeks. The after-Easter repeat target is still to be confirmed.
OBJECTIVE · BEHAVIOURAL
Shoppers choose Oven Crisps in the meal-deal snack slot instead of a big-brand fried crisp, and try the bag. Do not frame the work as a swap away from the loaves, which the client has said is a risk to avoid.
OBJECTIVE · ATTITUDINAL
To confirm: People believe a baked sourdough crisp can be properly crunchy, and feel it is a tasty, slightly special everyday treat, not a worthy compromise.
WHO WE ARE TALKING TO
Core: 18-35 office lunchers and students choosing a meal-deal crisp in seconds, often on promotion, and often snacking in place of a meal (62% of 16-34s). Secondary: parents buying for lunchboxes, plus food-curious shoppers. Not for diet-first shoppers or loyal fried-crisp heavy users. The client agreed to narrow 18-55 to this core.
COMPETITOR CONTEXT
Walkers leads, with 25.6% volume share of potato chips, and uses celebrity-led humour. Kettle owns 'hand-cooked, small batch' craft, Tyrrells sit in the same crafted space, Pringles own the familiar tube, Propercorn claims 'better for you', and popcorn is the alternative snack. The open space is a baked crisp with real bakery credentials and audible crunch that does not flee into health cues.
CONSUMER INSIGHT
The meal-deal crisp is the small treat that makes the lunch, and the crunch is the point. People want satisfaction without the greasy aftermath but will not trade taste for virtue: taste is the number one purchase driver.
SINGLE-MINDED PROPOSITION
Proper crunch.

REASONS TO BELIEVE
Made with the 80-year sourdough starter, so a real bakery makes the crisp.
40% less fat than the leading ready-salted fried crisps, lab tested, naming that comparator and stated per the same quantity of food. To confirm: cleared under CAP Code Section 15 before it runs.
No palm oil.
To confirm: proof of the crunch itself, such as a texture test or blind taste test against a fried crisp. None has been supplied, so the crunch stays a promise until it is.

RESPONSE · FEEL
To confirm: Delighted and a little cheeky, as if they have found a better snack that still feels like a treat.
RESPONSE · DO
To confirm: Pick up the bag in the meal-deal slot instead of a familiar fried one, and share the crunch.

MANDATORIES
Bag as hero, logo top left.
No real, recognisable faces.
Never show the product with alcohol.
Include 'Contains wheat. See pack for allergens.'
No compostable claim at launch.
The fat claim names its comparator and states the difference per the same quantity of food.
No bread or pub.
OPEN QUESTIONS
What is the 40% less fat claim measured against (which fried crisp, which test), and is it substantiated? — Answer: It is measured against the leading ready-salted fried crisps, lab tested. Say 40% less fat than the leading fried crisps, and name the comparator on pack.
When is compostable bag certification expected, and can we mention it before then? — Answer: Certification is expected in May, after launch. Do not mention compostable anywhere in the launch work. Say 'recyclable where facilities exist' only if legal agrees; otherwise say nothing about the bag.
What are the sales, distribution and trial targets for launch and after Easter? — Answer: Launch: listed in 3 of the big 5 supermarket meal deals by March, 2 million bags sold in the first 12 weeks. After Easter: repeat purchase above 25 percent of triers.
What is the budget, and does it include VAT and media? — Answer: The budget is 180,000 pounds ex VAT, media included. Treat that as one national poster burst, social, one 15 second film and sampling in about 200 stores.
Which supermarkets have listed the crisps and are they confirmed in meal deals? — Answer: Two supermarkets have confirmed meal-deal listings for March; the third is in talks. Do not name them in the work.
Is swapping people away from your own sourdough loaves a goal, or a risk to avoid? — Answer: A risk to avoid. The crisps are for the meal-deal snack slot, not a reason to leave the loaves. Do not show or mention bread as the thing to swap from.
Is the pub a real near-term target, or a later extension? — Answer: A later extension. The pub is out of scope for launch: no pub scenes, no pub media.
What is the 40% fat comparator, and what is the evidence? — Answer: Same answer as the fat question: the leading ready-salted fried crisps, lab tested.
When will compostable certification land? — Answer: Certification lands in May. No compostable claim at launch.
Is cannibalising the loaves acceptable? — Answer: Not acceptable. Do not frame the work as swapping away from the loaves.
Does the bag already carry a 'recyclable' line we can use, and with what exact wording? — Answer: No recyclable line on the bag yet. Say nothing about the bag's materials.
Are you happy to narrow the core audience to 18-35 lunchers and students, with parents secondary? — Answer: Yes: 18-35 lunchers and students are the core, parents secondary. And the founder has chosen the one line for all work: 'Proper crunch.' Use only that line, not the longer baked-not-fried one.
