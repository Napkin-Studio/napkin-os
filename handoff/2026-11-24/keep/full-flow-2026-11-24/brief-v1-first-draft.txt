Flat Out Good (working name)
CLIENT
Ferncliff Coffee Co., launching its new ready-to-drink Oat Flat White in a can.
PROJECT
Flat Out Good (working name)
BUDGET & SCOPE
Budget is TBC and smaller than last year's, which was also small. Scope: outdoor near train stations, TikTok and Instagram, a 15-second film that could also run in cinema, sampling at stations, and perhaps podcast sponsorship. Ideas are wanted on Thursday, with awareness wanted by Christmas and the launch in March. To confirm: whether the budget includes VAT and whether media sits inside it.
THE JOB
To confirm: Get busy people who grab an energy drink or queue for a café coffee to see a canned oat flat white as proper coffee, and pick it up.
WHERE WE ARE
Ferncliff is a hot coffee roaster that also supplies some cafés. It is moving into cold ready-to-drink coffee with a canned Oat Flat White. The product is cold and oat, and marketing calls it 'barista quality', which ops thinks is a stretch. The launch is in March, on a budget smaller than last year's, which was also small. The email also asks for everyone to know about it by Christmas, which conflicts with the March launch, so the dates need resolving before planning.
THE BARRIER
To confirm: Canned coffee tends to read as an energy-drink stand-in or a sugary latte, not real coffee, and 'barista quality' from a can is a claim people will doubt. The energy drink and the café stop are also habits already in place, so the can has to earn a switch.
PAGE 2 OF 4
OBJECTIVE · COMMERCIAL
Win share from energy drinks in convenience stores and forecourts, where the can sits in the same fridge and is bought on the move. Café share is secondary, and Ferncliff supplies some cafés, so the stance on cafés needs agreeing before we go after it. No target or period has been given, so both need the client's figures.
OBJECTIVE · BEHAVIOURAL
To confirm: People notice the can at a station or in a shop, try it through sampling or a first purchase, and then choose it over an energy drink or a café coffee on a regular journey. Shareability is welcome, but the aim is trial and repeat. A dance is not assumed and only makes sense if it comes naturally from the idea.
OBJECTIVE · ATTITUDINAL
To confirm: Move people from thinking of canned coffee as an energy-drink stand-in to thinking of Ferncliff Oat Flat White as real coffee that happens to be cold. A better view of the hot range may follow, but it is a by-product, not a second job.
WHO WE ARE TALKING TO
To confirm: Core: a 25-40 urban commuter or gym-goer. Picture her at 7.40am, on the platform with a train in four minutes. She wants a proper coffee but the café queue would make her miss it, so she reaches for whatever is in the station shop fridge. Secondary: students, school-run mums and night-shift nurses, who buy the same way, on the move and in a hurry. Not for children or pregnant women. The age range is unresolved (18-45 or 25-55) and needs the client's data to confirm.
COMPETITOR CONTEXT
Energy drinks such as Red Bull and Monster own the quick lift but not coffee taste. Supermarket iced coffees share the fridge. The café owns the proper flat white, and 30% of UK adults say they are loyal to one coffee shop or chain. The open space is real café coffee taste, with oat, and a lift without the energy-drink edge. Rival claims are unchecked.
CONSUMER INSIGHT
To confirm: She wants a proper coffee, but her morning only has room for what she can grab on the way, so she settles for a drink that does the job and tastes of nothing she chose. She has already accepted the compromise. What she lacks is a grab-and-go coffee that tastes like the café one she'd actually pick.

SINGLE-MINDED PROPOSITION
To confirm: The café flat white, in a can.
THE OBJECTION TO BEAT
To confirm: "It's just a canned coffee drink, and it won't taste like a proper flat white."
REASONS TO BELIEVE
Thirty years of Ferncliff roasting, the credibility behind the coffee.
The can itself, 100% recyclable aluminium (wording to be checked against recycling claim rules).
90 calories, a plain fact on the label.
RESPONSE · THINK
To confirm: "This is real coffee."
RESPONSE · FEEL
To confirm: Treated: a small premium moment in a rushed day. Avoid being preached at about caffeine, sustainability or being 'better' than other drinks.
RESPONSE · DO
To confirm: Try a can at a station sampling or in a corner shop or petrol station, and swap it in for the usual energy drink or café coffee.
TONE & WORLD
One stance: dry and unfussy, a confident wink and not a shout.
World: the real morning journey, platforms, shop fridges and gym bags, with the can as the hero.
Premium comes from restraint and craft, energy from pace, not volume.
Not: barista-theatre cliché, caffeine-hype, forced dance trends or cringe slang.

ROLE OF EACH MEDIUM
To confirm: Station outdoor: reaches the commuter on the route, just before the purchase.
Sampling at stations: the trial that proves the taste.
TikTok and Instagram: support, keeping it talked about.
To confirm: cinema and podcast, dropped unless the budget allows.
HOW WE WILL KNOW IT WORKED
To confirm: Judged by: the commercial and attitudinal objectives, once targets are set (To confirm).
Attribution: sampling areas against matched areas on sales (To confirm).
Before and after: 'real coffee' tracker baseline before the March launch, repeated after (To confirm).
In flight: sample take-up, social engagement, retailer listings.
The lag: sales share lands after launch; perception shift after the second wave.
MANDATORIES
The can is the hero.
Logo top right always.
No real people's faces on posters.
Do not show it being drunk while driving.
Include 'Contains caffeine. Not recommended for children or pregnant women.'
Do not claim B-Corp until certified.
Do not use 'no added sugar' until verified.
