FERNCLIFF COFFEE CO. · V1 · 23 NOV 2026
Flat Out Good
CLIENT
Ferncliff Coffee Co., a 30-year roaster launching its first canned Oat Flat White, which is cold and oat-based.
PROJECT
Flat Out Good
BUDGET & SCOPE
Working budget is £250k excl. VAT, media included, with lean production. Scope: outdoor near train stations, TikTok and Instagram, a 15-second film that could also run in cinema, station sampling and possible podcast sponsorship. March launch with a Christmas teaser only. Ideas due Thursday. Sampling will need to be scaled well below Costa's.
THE JOB
To confirm: Get the busy commuter who reaches for an energy drink to see a cold oat flat white as real coffee and pick it up instead.
WHERE WE ARE
Ferncliff is a 30-year hot roaster that also supplies some cafés. It is entering cold ready-to-drink coffee with a canned Oat Flat White. The launch is in March, with Christmas used only as a teaser phase. The budget is smaller than last year's, which was also small. 'Barista quality' is marketing's phrase and ops thinks it a stretch, so it stays out of copy. Costa RTD shows the category can grow quickly through sampling: it became a £8m brand within a year.
THE BARRIER
To confirm: Canned coffee reads as an energy-drink stand-in or a sugary latte, and she doubts a can can taste like a real flat white. The energy-drink grab is already a habit, so the can has to earn the switch at the fridge door.
PAGE 2 OF 5
OBJECTIVE · COMMERCIAL
Reach 8% share of the UK convenience RTD coffee fridge within 12 months of launch, read from retailer sales data. Energy drinks are the first source of share and cafés are secondary, because Ferncliff supplies some cafés and treats them as partners.
OBJECTIVE · BEHAVIOURAL
Notice the can at a station or shop, try it through sampling or a first purchase, then choose it over an energy drink on a regular journey. Shareability is welcome, but a dance is only pursued if it comes out of the idea.
OBJECTIVE · ATTITUDINAL
Shift canned coffee from energy-drink stand-in to real coffee that happens to be cold. Any halo on the hot range is a by-product, not a second job.
WHO WE ARE TALKING TO
Lead: 25-40 urban commuters and gym-goers grabbing a drink on the move. Picture her on the platform at 7.40am with a train in four minutes: she wants real coffee and has no time for the café queue. Secondary: students, school-run mums and night-shift nurses, who buy the same way, in a hurry. Not for children or pregnant women.
COMPETITOR CONTEXT
Red Bull and Monster own the quick lift, with Red Bull the best-known energy drink at 94% awareness. Supermarket iced coffees such as Tesco's share the fridge, and Costa RTD is the sampling-led benchmark. Cafés are partners and are never knocked. The open space is café-style oat coffee with a lift and no energy-drink edge. Rival claims are still to be checked.
CONSUMER INSIGHT
To confirm: She wants proper coffee, but the café queue would make her miss her train, so she grabs the energy drink because it's nearest and puts up with a lift she never chose. She shouldn't have to choose between fast and good.
SINGLE-MINDED PROPOSITION
To confirm: A café-style flat white, in a can, that is as good as the real thing.

THE OBJECTION TO BEAT
To confirm: "It's just a canned coffee drink and won't taste like a proper flat white."
REASONS TO BELIEVE
Thirty years of Ferncliff roasting, said plainly and never as 'barista quality'.
Station sampling as the taste test. Take-up and feedback are the evidence to collect, as there is no Ferncliff result yet.
90 calories, stated as a plain fact.
To confirm: Rainforest Alliance beans, only if the certification is current.
To confirm: 100% recyclable aluminium, only with evidence held; the ASA reads it as 'can be recycled', not 'will be'.
No 'no added sugar' claim until verified, and no B-Corp claim.
RESPONSE · THINK
To confirm: "This is real coffee, and it's good."
RESPONSE · FEEL
To confirm: Treated: a small premium moment in a rushed day, with no preaching.
RESPONSE · DO
To confirm: Try a can at station sampling or in a corner shop or forecourt, and swap it in for the usual energy drink.
TONE & WORLD
Playful but premium, with a dry, confident wink.
Calm with pace: energy without volume.
Set in real journeys such as platforms, shop fridges and gym bags, with the can as hero.
Not barista theatre, caffeine hype or forced dance trends, and not cringe.

ROLE OF EACH MEDIUM
To confirm: Station outdoor: reaches the commuter just before the fridge.
Sampling: answers the taste doubt directly, as Costa's sampling-led launch showed, scaled down to fit.
Social: shows the can in real journeys, where younger drinkers often try something new.
To confirm: 15-second film, only if the budget allows after sampling and outdoor.

Attribution: To confirm: sampling areas against matched areas.
Before and after: To confirm: a 'real coffee' tracker wave before launch.
In flight: sample take-up, listings, social engagement.

MANDATORIES
The can is the hero.
Logo top right always.
No real people's faces on posters.
Do not show it being drunk while driving.
Include 'Contains caffeine. Not recommended for children or pregnant women.' To confirm: the wording against GB rules, which exempt drinks with 'coffee' in the name.
No 'barista quality'.
No 'no added sugar' until verified.
No B-Corp claim; the Pending B Corp logo cannot be used in print or on packaging.
Never knock cafés.
