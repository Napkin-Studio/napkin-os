FERNCLIFF COFFEE CO. · V1 · 23 NOV 2026
Flat Out Good
CLIENT
Ferncliff Coffee Co., a 30-year roaster launching its first canned Oat Flat White.
PROJECT
Flat Out Good
BUDGET & SCOPE
Working budget £250k excl. VAT, media included, with lean production. Scope: station outdoor, TikTok and Instagram, a 15-second film that could also run in cinema, station sampling, and possible podcast sponsorship. March launch with a Christmas teaser phase only. Ideas due Thursday.
THE JOB
To confirm: Get the busy commuter who grabs an energy drink to see a canned oat flat white as proper coffee, and pick it up instead.
WHERE WE ARE
Ferncliff is a 30-year hot roaster that also supplies some cafés. It is entering cold ready-to-drink coffee with a canned Oat Flat White that is cold and made with oat. The launch is in March, with Christmas used only for a teaser phase, not as the awareness deadline. The budget is smaller than last year's, which was also small. 'Barista quality' is marketing's phrase, ops thinks it is a stretch, and it is not to be used in copy.
THE BARRIER
To confirm: Canned coffee reads as an energy-drink stand-in or a sugary latte, and she doubts a can can taste like a real flat white. The energy-drink grab is already a habit, so the can has to earn the switch at the fridge door.
PAGE 2 OF 5
OBJECTIVE · COMMERCIAL
Reach 8% share of the UK convenience RTD coffee fridge within 12 months of launch, tracked by retailer sales data. Energy drinks are the first source of share and cafés are secondary, because Ferncliff supplies some cafés and treats them as partners.
OBJECTIVE · BEHAVIOURAL
To confirm: Notice the can at a station or shop, try it through sampling or a first purchase, then choose it over an energy drink on a regular journey. Shareability is welcome, but a dance is not assumed and is only worth pursuing if it comes out of the idea.
OBJECTIVE · ATTITUDINAL
To confirm: Shift canned coffee from energy-drink stand-in to real coffee that happens to be cold. Any halo on the hot range is a by-product, not a second job.
WHO WE ARE TALKING TO
Lead: 25-40 urban commuters and gym-goers, grabbing a drink on the move. Picture her on the platform at 7.40am with a train in four minutes: she wants a proper coffee, the café queue would make her miss it, so she reaches into the station shop fridge. Secondary: students, school-run mums and night-shift nurses, who buy the same way, in a hurry. Not for children or pregnant women.
COMPETITOR CONTEXT
Red Bull and Monster own the quick lift, not coffee taste. Tesco's iced coffees share the fridge, and Costa and Starbucks bring café credentials. Cafés own the flat white and are partners, never knocked. The open space is café-style, oat coffee taste with a lift and no energy-drink edge. Rival claims are still to be checked.
CONSUMER INSIGHT
To confirm: She wants a proper coffee, but the café queue would make her miss her train. So she grabs the energy drink, because it's nearest and quickest, and puts up with a lift she never chose. She shouldn't have to choose between fast and good.
SINGLE-MINDED PROPOSITION
It tastes like a café flat white, in a can.

THE OBJECTION TO BEAT
To confirm: "It's just a canned coffee drink and won't taste like a proper flat white."
REASONS TO BELIEVE
Thirty years of Ferncliff roasting: the craft behind the can, said plainly and never as 'barista quality'.
To confirm: station sampling as the taste test. This is a trial to run, not a result we hold. Sample take-up and feedback are the evidence to collect, and there is no Ferncliff result yet.
90 calories, stated as a plain fact.
RESPONSE · THINK
To confirm: "This is real coffee, and it's good."
RESPONSE · FEEL
To confirm: Treated: a small premium moment in a rushed day, with no preaching.
RESPONSE · DO
To confirm: Try a can at station sampling or in a corner shop or forecourt, and swap it in for the usual energy drink.
TONE & WORLD
Dry, unfussy, a confident wink: playful but premium.
Calm with pace, energy without volume.
Set in real journeys: platforms, shop fridges, gym bags, with the can as hero.
Not barista theatre, caffeine hype or forced dance trends.
ROLE OF EACH MEDIUM
To confirm: Station outdoor: reaches the commuter on route, just before the fridge.
Sampling: lets her taste a café-style flat white, the answer to the taste doubt.
Social: shows the can in real commutes and gym bags.
To confirm: 15-second film, only if the £250k allows.

Judged by: 8% convenience RTD coffee share in 12 months via retailer data.
Attribution: To confirm: sampling areas against matched areas.
Before and after: To confirm: 'real coffee' tracker before and after launch.
In flight: sample take-up, listings, social engagement.

MANDATORIES
The can is the hero.
Logo top right always.
No real people's faces on posters.
Do not show it being drunk while driving.
Include the legal line 'Contains caffeine. Not recommended for children or pregnant women.' To confirm: the exact wording against GB caffeine labelling rules, which exempt drinks with 'coffee' in the name.
No 'barista quality'.
No 'no added sugar' until verified.
No B-Corp claim until certified; the Pending B Corp logo cannot be used in print or on packaging.
Never knock cafés.
OPEN QUESTIONS
Which date matters most: awareness by Christmas or a March launch? — Answer: Launch in March is the date that matters. Christmas is a teaser and awareness build, not the goal.
What is the budget, does it include VAT, and does media sit inside it? — Answer: Working budget is 250k GBP excl. VAT, media included. Production must be lean.
What share, sales or distribution targets and measures of success do you hold? — Answer: Target: 8% share of the UK convenience RTD coffee fridge within 12 months of launch, tracked by retailer sales data.
Is 'no added sugar' verified, and can 'barista quality' be used? — Answer: No added sugar is not verified yet; do not claim it. Do not use the words barista quality in any copy.
What is the agreed age range and priority audience? — Answer: Lead audience is 25-40 urban commuters and gym-goers; students, school-run mums and night-shift nurses are secondary.
How should we treat the cafés you supply while winning café share? — Answer: Treat the cafes we supply as partners: never knock cafes. We win from energy drinks first; cafes are secondary.
Which line is chosen: 'Flat out good' or the longer 'Really...' line? — Answer: Use Flat out good as the line. Keep Really coffee as a secondary sign-off only.
