FERNCLIFF COFFEE CO. · V1 · 23 NOV 2026
Flat Out Good
CLIENT
Ferncliff Coffee Co., a 30-year roaster, launching its first canned Oat Flat White.
PROJECT
Flat Out Good
BUDGET & SCOPE
Budget is TBC and smaller than last year's; ask whether any figure includes VAT and whether media sits inside it. Scope: outdoor near train stations, TikTok and Instagram, a 15-second film that could also run in cinema, station sampling and possible podcast sponsorship. March launch with a Christmas teaser only. Ideas due Thursday.
THE JOB
To confirm: Get the busy commuter who reaches for an energy drink to see a cold oat flat white as real coffee, and pick it up instead.
WHERE WE ARE
Ferncliff is a hot roaster that also supplies some cafés. It enters cold ready-to-drink coffee in March with a canned Oat Flat White, on a smaller budget than last year's. UK RTD coffee was worth an estimated USD 490.97 million in 2025 and is forecast to grow 6.08% a year. Costa RTD became an £8m brand within a year, helped by sampling.
THE BARRIER
The energy-drink grab at the fridge door is already a habit, and the can has to earn the switch.
PAGE 2 OF 5
OBJECTIVE · COMMERCIAL
Win 8% of the UK convenience RTD coffee fridge within 12 months of launch, read from retailer sales data. Energy drinks are the first source of share and cafés are secondary, as Ferncliff supplies some cafés and treats them as partners. The 8% figure is to be confirmed by the client.
OBJECTIVE · BEHAVIOURAL
To confirm: Notice the can at a station or shop, try it through sampling or a first purchase, then choose it over an energy drink on a regular journey. Shareability is welcome only if it comes from the idea, not a bolted-on dance.
OBJECTIVE · ATTITUDINAL
To confirm: Canned coffee shifts from energy-drink stand-in to real coffee that happens to be cold. Any halo on the hot range is a by-product, not a second job.
WHO WE ARE TALKING TO
Lead: 25-40 urban commuters and gym-goers grabbing a drink on the move, such as someone on the platform at 7.40am with a train in four minutes who wants real coffee without the café queue. Secondary: students, school-run mums and night-shift nurses, who buy the same way, in a hurry. Not for children or pregnant women. The age range still needs data to confirm it.
COMPETITOR CONTEXT
Red Bull and Monster own the quick lift, and Red Bull is the best-known energy drink at 94% awareness. Supermarket iced coffees, such as Tesco's, share the fridge. Costa RTD is the sampling-led benchmark and Starbucks RTD is expanding its UK chilled range. Cafés are partners and never knocked. The open space is café-style oat coffee with a lift and no energy-drink edge. What rivals claim still needs checking.
CONSUMER INSIGHT
To confirm: She wants proper coffee, but the café queue would make her miss her train, so she grabs the energy drink because it's nearest and puts up with a lift she never chose. She shouldn't have to choose between fast and good.
SINGLE-MINDED PROPOSITION
Flat out good: real café-style oat coffee, in a can, as quick as grabbing an energy drink.
PAGE 3 OF 5
THE OBJECTION TO BEAT
To confirm: "It's just a canned coffee drink and won't taste like a proper flat white."
REASONS TO BELIEVE
Thirty years of Ferncliff roasting, stated plainly.
Station sampling as the taste test; take-up and feedback are the evidence to collect, as there is no Ferncliff result yet.
90 calories, stated as a plain fact.
RESPONSE · THINK
To confirm: "This is real coffee, and it's good."
RESPONSE · FEEL
To confirm: Treated: a small premium moment in a rushed day, with no preaching.
RESPONSE · DO
To confirm: Try a can at station sampling or in a corner shop or forecourt, and swap it in for the usual energy drink.
TONE & WORLD
Playful but premium, with a dry, confident wink.
Calm with pace: energy without volume.
Set in real journeys, with the can as hero.
Not barista theatre, caffeine hype or forced dance trends, and not cringe.
ROLE OF EACH MEDIUM
Station outdoor: reaches the commuter just before the fridge.
Sampling: answers the taste doubt directly, scaled down from Costa's to fit budget.
Social: shows the can in real journeys, where younger drinkers try new things.
To confirm: 15-second film, only if budget allows.

HOW WE WILL KNOW IT WORKED
To confirm: Judged by share of the convenience RTD coffee fridge against the 8% objective, via retailer data.
Attribution: To confirm: sampling areas against matched areas.
Before and after: To confirm: a 'real coffee' tracker wave before launch.
In flight: sample take-up, listings, social engagement.
Lag: share lands after launch.

MANDATORIES
The can is the hero.
Logo top right always.
No real people's faces on posters.
Do not show it being drunk while driving.
Include 'Contains caffeine. Not recommended for children or pregnant women.' To confirm: the wording against GB rules, which exempt drinks with 'coffee' in the name from the high-caffeine label.
No 'barista quality'.
No 'no added sugar' until verified.
No B-Corp claim; the Pending B Corp logo cannot be used on packaging or in print.
Never knock cafés.
OPEN QUESTIONS
Which date matters most: awareness by Christmas or a March launch? — Answer: Launch in March is the date that matters. Christmas is a teaser and awareness build, not the goal.
What is the budget, does it include VAT, and does media sit inside it? — Answer: Working budget is 250k GBP excl. VAT, media included. Production must be lean.
What share, sales or distribution targets and measures of success do you hold? — Answer: Target: 8% share of the UK convenience RTD coffee fridge within 12 months of launch, tracked by retailer sales data.
Is 'no added sugar' verified, and can 'barista quality' be used? — Answer: No added sugar is not verified yet; do not claim it. Do not use the words barista quality in any copy.
What is the agreed age range and priority audience? — Answer: Lead audience is 25-40 urban commuters and gym-goers; students, school-run mums and night-shift nurses are secondary.
How should we treat the cafés you supply while winning café share? — Answer: Treat the cafes we supply as partners: never knock cafes. We win from energy drinks first; cafes are secondary.
Which line is chosen: 'Flat out good' or the longer 'Really...' line? — Answer: Use Flat out good as the line. Keep Really coffee as a secondary sign-off only.
