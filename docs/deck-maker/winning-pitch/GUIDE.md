# What wins a pitch: a guide for the Deck Maker

Date: 2026-10-16. Every claim is followed by its source in brackets. Tags: [P] primary, [S] secondary (a report of the study, original not read), [O] opinion (practitioner view, not data), [X] extrapolation (study is from another domain). Where we have no primary source the claim is marked opinion. Full list and caveats: SOURCES.md.

## 1. What a pitch is for

A pitch does not need to be the best idea. It has to make one buyer group choose us with confidence, and survive being retold by people who were not in the room.

- Buyers choose on thinking first, then team. Scopen's marketer interviews: what they look for is quality of personnel and, "most importantly", quality of thinking [S: Bizcommunity reporting Scopen Agency Scope 2023/24, https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/scopen-chemistry-between-agency-and-marketer-becomes-more-important-781682a].
- 75% of client teams said the winning agency showed true understanding of the brand; asking questions, offering solutions and raising new challenges featured in almost 50% of successful pitches [S: Campaign Canada reporting The GO! Network feedback on 250+ calls, https://www.campaigncanada.ca/article/five-factors-making-or-breaking-an-agency-pitch/4p6dbn0gryk3k4b1bkw1zash40; original dataset not found].
- A pitch is often won before it begins, through research into the client's business and decision-making; "Chemistry matters, but it can't come at the expense of substance." [O: AAR Group, "What wins a pitch", 2026, https://aargroup.co.uk/?p=3449]
- The decision is not purely arithmetic. WFA/EACA: a decision made purely by adding up numbers is unlikely to be as successful as a balance between scoring and subjective assessment (chemistry) [P: WFA/EACA guidelines, 2009, https://archive.soz.si/sites/default/files/wfa_eaca_pitch_guidelines.pdf].
- The idea often never runs: only 25% of pitch-winning ideas are executed, per AAR statistics quoted by the IPA/ISBA guide [P for the quote, S for the AAR figure: https://ipa.co.uk/media/7916/finding-an-agency-final.pdf; AAR original not found]. So strategy and the working relationship are the real product.
- Clients decide from recall. They find it hard to remember who said what, and the last agency to present can have a major advantage [P: WFA/EACA 11.3.3-11.3.4, same URL]. Serial-position effects in juries are real in music and sport judging (later slots score higher) [X: Bruine de Bruin 2005, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15698823/]. No procurement-specific study was found.
- Group discussion under-repeats reasons only one member holds, so the "why us" must be short, repeated in the same words, and easy to relay [X: Stasser and Titus 1985, https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.48.6.1467; accessed via secondary description; the committee application is opinion].

## 2. Gate first, then score

Format and compliance failures lose bids before quality is read.

- GAO upheld the Navy ignoring every page beyond the page limit; required information sat on later pages, so the proposal was technically unacceptable. "Clearly stated solicitation technical requirements are considered material to the needs of the government, and a proposal that fails to conform to such material terms is technically unacceptable" [P: GAO B-418318, JJ Global Services, 2020, https://www.gao.gov/assets/b-418318.pdf]. US federal context, principle general.
- Brent: a score of 0 on any question, or under 3 on two or more, can exclude the whole bid [P: https://democracy.brent.gov.uk/documents/s63020/Appendix%202%20-%20Invitation%20to%20Tender%20ITT%20evaluation%20criteria%20and%20scoring%20matrix.pdf]. In Brent's 2023 award report a bidder scoring 2 on three quality questions was excluded and its price "was not scored" [P: https://democracy.brent.gov.uk/documents/s149902/Appendix 2 - Evaluation Grid.pdf].
- Wiltshire: mandatory requirements are pass/fail, "Any fails will lead to rejection of the Tender" [P: https://cms.wiltshire.gov.uk/documents/s135412/Appendix%201%20-%20Tender%20Evaluation%20Procedure%20with%20Worked%20Example.pdf; PDF metadata 2017].
- Scope fidelity: WFA/EACA say that if the client asked only for evaluation and strategies, creative ideas should be refused, because showing creative changes the judgement dynamics [P: WFA/EACA section 14, https://archive.soz.si/sites/default/files/wfa_eaca_pitch_guidelines.pdf].
- Award criteria must relate to the subject matter of the contract, so generic company history earns no marks [P: UK Government, Assessing Competitive Tenders, 2025, https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/690cb65edad669f061d46b5a/Guidance_-_Assessing_Competitive_Tenders_FINAL_pdf_links_.docx.pdf].

Rule for the Deck Maker: gate = every requirement mapped, no rule broken, inside the page and word limits, nothing unrequested. A fail is reported before any score.

## 3. What wins, by kind

### 3.1 Agency pitch (strategy and ideas to a marketer)

Weighting evidence (diagnosis outweighs decoration):
- Cannes Creative Strategy Lions: 30% interpretation of the challenge, 30% insight, 20% creative idea, 20% outcome [P: https://www.canneslions.com/awards/lions/creative-strategy/what-you-need-to-know, 2026].
- Effie: 23.3% Challenge/Context/Objectives, 23.3% Insights and Strategy, 23.3% Bringing the Strategy and Idea to Life, 30% Results [P: 2025 Effective Entry Guide, https://www.effie.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2025_Effective-Entry-Guide.pdf]. For a forward-looking pitch, "results" become targets and a measurement plan [our inference].
- Cannes Creative Effectiveness is 25/25/50 idea/strategy/results [P: https://www.canneslions.com/awards/lions/creative-effectiveness/what-you-need-to-know]. That is the weighting for a case-study deck, not a pitch.
- Brent's ITT (a design brief, not marketing) weighted team and project management 23% above design concept 15% [P: Brent URL above]. Delivery team counts.
- IPA/ISBA name four example criteria buckets: strategy, creativity, culture/chemistry, commercials, and tell clients to share relative weighting. They give no weights [P: https://ipa.co.uk/media/7916/finding-an-agency-final.pdf]. TrinityP3: no more than 10 questions, a 1-5 scale with defined meanings, written comments with every score; no standard weightings found [P: https://trinityp3.com/pitching-support/essentials-of-agency-pitch-scorecards].

What clients say wins and loses (all secondary or opinion):
- Wins: demonstrated understanding, new challenges raised, research done (24.6% said the agency had clearly done research) [S: Campaign Canada / GO! Network, URL in section 1].
- Loses: a senior contact overwhelming the call (28.3% of rejections after chemistry calls); not meeting the day-to-day team (15% of unsuccessful pitches); lack of chemistry (about 20.5%); commercials and affordability (about 18%) [S: same].
- Marketers and agencies disagree: 42% of marketers said value for cost is influential vs 13% of agencies; 70% of marketers expect primary research; 36% of agencies say they always rethink the client's whole strategy vs 7% of marketers who say they see it [S: Bulldog Reporter on Vennli's 2018 survey of 200 US professionals, https://www.agilitypr.com/pr-news/marketing-news/marketers-now-expect-primary-research-new-agency-pitches/; vendor survey, dated].
- Agencies' own post-loss checklist: talked about themselves too much, unclear USP, did not demonstrate value, weaker team members, not united as a team [O: TrinityP3, https://www.trinityp3.com/pitching-support/questions-ask-when-lose-pitch/].
- Price is there "to reinforce the case an agency has already made - not to make it" [O: AAR, https://aargroup.co.uk/?p=3449].

Process facts: best practice is a final pitch list of up to 3 agencies (4 with the incumbent); a chemistry meeting and a tissue session exist as separate formats, and some wins are decided with no finished creative [P: IPA/ISBA 2019, https://ipa.co.uk/media/7916/finding-an-agency-final.pdf]. A "credentials/chemistry" deck is a different artefact from a full pitch; WFA/EACA say keep credentials very short and show the team that will do the work [P: WFA/EACA 10.1-10.2].

### 3.2 Tender response (public buyer, scored against published criteria)

- Quality outweighs price: HSE creative advertising framework 70/30 [P: https://ojeu.com/ojdblnk/view-notice.php?id=3998541, 2024]; Failte Ireland B2C Digital and Content Agency 60/40, four tenders received [P: https://ted.europa.eu/udl?uri=TED%3ANOTICE%3A427015-2026%3APDF%3AEN%3AHTML, 2026]; Brent 60/40; Wiltshire 70/30 (quality 60% written, 40% live demonstrations, so demos about 28% of total) [P: URLs in section 2].
- Published notices can hide the detail: Enterprise Ireland's notice listed award criterion "Quality" only, with no weights [P: https://www.etenders.gov.ie/epps/notices/downloadNoticeForES.do?resourceId=3360089&noticeType=prompt.notice.name.29]. Take weights from the user's ITT; never assume.
- UK central government: award goes to the most economically advantageous tender, not the cheapest; cost is to be secondary to quality in complex outsourcing; relative price scoring is discouraged; minimum 10% social value weighting when any social value outcome is included [P: Sourcing Playbook 2023, https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/64901fcc5f7bb700127fac5e/Sourcing_Playbook_Final.pdf].
- Bands: Scottish Government suggests a 0/25/50/75/100 scale and says method, weights and sub-criteria must be disclosed and not changed [P: https://procurementjourney.scot/route-2/develop-documents/exclusion-selection-and-award-criteria/award-criteria, 2025]. Brent's 5 needs "exceeds expectation" and "full confidence and no concerns"; a 3 is "some good evidence" [P: Brent URL].
- Weights inside sections vary, so spend pages on the highest-weighted sub-questions (Brent: 20/20/10 inside a 50% section) [P: Brent 2023 award report].
- 53% of Scottish suppliers say they can use previous feedback; 64% say they can respond effectively to evaluation criteria; 24% got no feedback on any bid in two years [P: https://www.gov.scot/publications/public-procurement-survey-suppliers-2024/pages/5/, 2025]. A criteria-mapped structure is therefore a differentiator.

Build rule: one section per scored criterion, in the buyer's order, space proportional to weight, a visible response map, social value scored separately where required [our inference from the above].

### 3.3 Investor deck (seed and pre-seed)

- Sequoia's own template order: purpose, problem, solution, why now, market potential, competition/alternatives, business model, team, financials, vision. "The best companies almost always have a clear why now?" and for competition "Show that you have a plan to win." [P: https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/]
- Reading time: just under three minutes on average in 2022; failed decks were abandoned at 2m13s; 320 decks [S: TechCrunch reporting DocSend, 2022, https://techcrunch.com/2022/09/22/science-of-pitch-decks/amp]. 3m44s in 2015 [S: https://techcrunch.com/2015/06/08/lessons-from-a-study-of-perfect-pitch-decks-vcs-spend-an-average-of-3-minutes-44-seconds-on-them; the sample was small, per DocSend's co-founder]. The deck must work as a skim.
- Successful decks averaged 20 pages vs 17 for failed and matched Sequoia's order; investors spent about 22 seconds on the purpose slide of successful decks and about 61 seconds on the opening product page of failed ones; 56% of time on product, financials and team in failed decks vs 26% in successful [S: DocSend via TechCrunch, 2019, https://techcrunch.com/2019/04/12/data-tells-us-that-investors-love-a-good-story]. So length is not the lever; lead with purpose, keep product light.
- DocSend research page: roughly 20 pages at about 50 words a slide; successful decks had no table of contents, put product and why now earlier, and more often included competition [P-ish vendor page: https://www.dropbox.com/resources/docsend-pitch-deck-research; undated].
- Missing sections lose: in DocSend's 2022 set none of the unsuccessful decks included financials; only two-thirds of 2020 pre-seed decks included an ask [S: https://techcrunch.com/2020/03/03/docsends-new-pre-seed-data-shows-how-many-founders-you-should-have-and-how-many-investors-you-should-meet/].
- Clarity: Seibel (YC) says open with what the company does; strip jargon, marketing speak and ambiguous words like "platform"; end with what you want [P: https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company/, 2016].
- Kawasaki's 10 slides, 20 minutes, 30pt font is opinion for a presented pitch, not a study [O: https://guykawasaki.com/the-only-10-slides-you-need-in-your-pitch/]. It conflicts with the DocSend 20-page average; split presented spine from sent deck.
- Slidebean's Airbnb teardown is secondary and low confidence; we use none of its numbers [S: https://slidebean.com/blog/startups/airbnb-pitch-deck].

## 4. How winners are built

### 4.1 Order

Answer first: situation and quantified objective, insight, the one idea, plan, proof, team, investment, ask.
- Cannes' own strategy deck order: original brief, research methods and findings, how strategy developed, data sources, visuals, implementation roadmap; capped at 10 pages [P: Cannes Creative Strategy URL].
- IPA: judged on the chain of logic from challenge to solution to proof, "unmissable and unassailable"; write the opening last as a teaser consistent with the evidence [P: https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/six-steps-to-the-effectiveness-awards-entry-finish-line/, 2018].
- Effie: the insight must be more than a fact, "what the data point means", unique to the brand, tied to human behaviour, and link through strategy to idea, execution and results [P: Effie 2025 guide]. Judges want a crisp strategic idea, not the tactical execution.
- Effie: weak context weakens the whole entry; objectives must be specific, measurable and not retrofitted [P: same].

### 4.2 Length and density

No primary study shows that slide count predicts winning (searched, not found) [gap]. What is evidenced:
- Fit the slot and keep a leave-behind. WFA/EACA: agencies must stick to allotted time; the presentation should select the most important aspects, with background in the document [P: WFA/EACA 11.3].
- Working memory holds about four chunks [P: Cowan 2001, https://memory.psych.missouri.edu/assets/doc/articles/2001/cowan-bbs-2001.pdf]. Kosslyn et al.: max four bullets, max two lines per bullet, text over 20pt; 91% of audited slideshows broke the bullet limit [P: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00230/full].
- Assertion-evidence slides (sentence headline plus visual) gave higher causal-mechanism accuracy than topic-plus-bullets (d = 0.60, 110 engineering students; 21.2 vs 41.5 words per slide) [P, X: Garner and Alley 2013, https://www.ijee.ie/articles/Vol29-6/23_ijee2791ns.pdf]. Students, not buyers.
- Extraneous material hurts learning (coherence effect, median 0.90); duplicating narration as on-screen text hurts (0.69) [P, X: Mayer and Moreno 2003, https://uky.edu/~gmswan3/544/9_ways_to_reduce_CL.pdf]. Narrated animations, not live decks.
- Practitioners: "Say. Fewer. Words."; cut the deck in half [O: The Drum 2025, https://www.thedrum.com/news/2025/04/24/the-great-pitch-theater-debate-should-agencies-go-big-or-keep-it-simple].
- Napkin's 18 presented slides and two minutes a slide are unsourced agency rules of thumb [repo: engine/deck/spec.py, ADR 0024]. Keep them as defaults, label them as such.

### 4.3 The idea

- One idea, stated in one sentence, shown in the client's real context. Waste Creative won a Riot Games pitch within minutes without a slide by simulating Riot's Twitter feed [O: The Drum 2025, same URL]. Most pitches are remembered for one thing, maybe two [O: same].
- Peak-end: memory of an experience tracks its peak and last minutes, not length [X: Redelmeier and Kahneman 1996, medical procedures, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8857625/]. Use it as a design heuristic: one vivid peak, a strong close.
- A concrete character-and-tension story as the emotional peak has weak support (lab video work) [X: Zak 2015, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26034526/]. Do not quote oxytocin figures.

### 4.4 The plan and proof

- Effie's plan section asks why each channel was chosen over others, the customer path, evolution over time, and an investment overview; judges read the written case before the creative reel [P: Effie 2025 guide].
- IPA: an ROI figure is not required; ROMI measures efficiency and can be inflated by cutting spend; winners are chosen on strength of proof [P: https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/6-ipa-effectiveness-awards-myths/].
- Effie: "When key metrics are withheld without explanation, judges may assume it is because the results were weak", and judges value a real answer to "what other factors could have contributed" [P: Effie 2025 guide]. Cannes Effectiveness makes a "Discounting Factors" answer mandatory [P: Cannes URL]. For a pitch: a measurement plan with KPIs, baselines, and how success is separated from other causes [IPA blog on rebutting alternative explanations: https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/why-an-ipa-effectiveness-awards-submission-is-not-just-any-award-submission/].

### 4.5 The team, price and ask

- Show the people who will do the day-to-day work; senior leaders attend without dominating [O: AAR; S: GO! Network]. IPA/ISBA: senior decision makers involved day to day should attend all key stages [P].
- Three options: Blair Enns says always put three options in a proposal, because it changes the question from "should we buy this?" to "which is best value?" [O: https://archive.davidairey.com/dealing-with-designers/pricing-creativity-blair-enns/, 2018]. Choice overload shows fewer purchases with 24-30 options than 6 [P, X: Iyengar and Lepper 2000, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11138768/; later literature is mixed]. Rule: one recommendation, at most 2-3 options that differ in scope.
- Value is framed as whole-life value; relative price scoring is discouraged [P: Sourcing Playbook].
- Budget placement: "present the budget first" and "pitches are almost never won in the room" are opposing opinions [O, low confidence: The Drum 2025]. Test, do not assume.
- End with an explicit ask (Seibel: end with what you want) [P: YC 2016].

## 5. Common ways pitches lose

1. Format and mandatory-item failures (section 2).
2. Opening with the agency or product. Failed investor decks dwell on product [S: DocSend 2019]; failed agency pitches talk about themselves [O: TrinityP3].
3. Vague or retrofitted objectives [P: Effie].
4. Insight that is a restated fact [P: Effie].
5. Senior-only team, no working team [S: GO! Network].
6. Missing sections: financials, competition, ask [S: DocSend]; no exit strategy or no evidence of need in public funding bids [P, funding-bid not agency: Slough Borough Council, https://slough.gov.uk/community-development/write-funding-bid/8].
7. Unsupported claims, and category rules broken. The ASA upheld complaints against Prime Time Lager ads ("EASY ON THE WAIST", "63% FEWER CARBS") although the advertiser said an agency told it the ad was cleared [P: https://asa.org.uk/rulings/the-innovative-brewing-company-ltd-g24-1267427-the-innovative-brewing-company-ltd.html, 2025]. The page does not say the defence affected the outcome.
8. Over-claiming a total rethink that clients do not perceive [S: Vennli].
9. Vague client briefs the deck never corrects: requests for proposals are "often lengthy but full of questions"; clients "tend to remain vague" [S: MediaPost on an ID Comms/4A's study, 2018, https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/328560/media-agency-pitch-process-lacks-clarity-on-client.html]. State assumptions.
10. Over-length. The Drum practitioners: reaching the end of your deck means you will lose [O].
11. Being one of many: the whole review process costs about $1.2M with an incumbent, so clients want simpler staged processes [S, low: ANA/4A's, https://www.ana.net/miccontent/show/id/rr-2022-09-agency-search-simplification; full report not read].

## 6. What to borrow from award cases

- From Effie: the four-part spine (context, insight/strategy, idea in action, proof) and its question on other factors.
- From Cannes Creative Strategy: put diagnosis first (60%), cap the deck at about 10 pages for a strategy response, and make the roadmap explicit.
- From the IPA: chain of logic as its own check; write the summary last.
- From D&AD: no verified primary source was read, so nothing is borrowed [see SOURCES.md].
- Caveat: award cases are retrospective and judged on results. A pitch has targets, not results. Nothing here shows that award-style structure wins pitches; it is the best available proxy for how experts judge strategy.

## 7. How we will judge our own decks

LLM judges mislead without controls:
- Position bias: GPT-4 was consistent under swapped order only 65.0% of the time; reverse the order and treat flips as ties [P: Zheng et al., https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685v4].
- Verbosity: weaker judges fell for padded lists in 91.3% of cases [P, medium confidence; figure read via summary: same URL]. Length-control the scores [P: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04475].
- Judge writes evidence before the score [P: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926].
- A panel from different model families beat a single large judge [P: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796]. Models favour their own output [P: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076].
- Reference-guided judging cut error from 70% to 15% on checkable answers, so check numbers in code against the brief [P: Zheng et al.].
- Many binary checks beat a holistic number (PresentBench uses about 54 per instance) [P: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07244].
- None of these judge studies validated against real pitch outcomes [gap].
