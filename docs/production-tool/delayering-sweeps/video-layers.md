# Can layers carry into video?

Sweep: layered video and layer-aware video editing, 2025–2026. Written 2026-10-23.
Note: the brief at `scratchpad/delayering/brief.md` was absent when this agent ran (the directory was empty), so this sweep works from the angle text alone. The web-search budget ran out near the end; the last gaps (Adobe helpx pages, Runway help centre, Kling's own O3 docs) are marked as secondary or unverified.

## Short answer

1. **No practical route exists in 2026 to take a generated clip apart into movable layers and put it back together.** The research does exist (Generative Omnimatte, OmnimatteZero, EasyOmnimatte, LayerFlow) and it produces real per-object RGBA layers with shadows and reflections, but every released version runs for minutes per clip on a 48 GB+ GPU, needs object masks as input, and is either non-commercial (OmnimatteZero), unreleased (EasyOmnimatte, LayerFlow) or a research reimplementation that admits it does not match the paper (gen-omnimatte-public, Apache-2.0). None is a hosted API.
2. **What exists is in-context editing: you say or show what should change and the model re-renders the whole clip.** On the Runway API that is `aleph2` ($0.28/s, input 2–30 s at up to 1080p, up to 5 keyframe images, no reference images), `klingO3_standard|pro|4k` video_to_video ($0.13/$0.17/$0.43 per second of input, source clip at most 10 s, image references and "elements" addressed by tag), `seedance2_5` with `mode: "edit"`, and the `product_swap` recipe (212 credits for 4 s at 720p plus 36 credits per extra second; reference video 1.8–15 s; 1–10 new product images). None of these returns an alpha channel: Runway's own SDK says the alpha in aleph2's ProRes 4444 output "is present but fully opaque".
3. **So delayering stays a stills feature.** The video analogue is not "move this layer" but "re-render this clip with this layer's content changed", with the change described by text, by a reference image, or by an edited frame. Swapping a product, replacing a subject, removing a distraction and relighting are the edit types that vendors advertise and that fit a layer vocabulary. A positional move ("the bottle 20 % to the left") has no first-class control anywhere; it would be a text instruction with no guarantee.
4. **Two side routes are real but narrow.** (a) Alpha-channel generation: Wan-Alpha (Apache-2.0, Wan2.1-14B, text-to-video only, I2V still on the to-do list, 8-GPU example) generates a transparent foreground clip to composite over a background clip; TransPixeler does the same on CogVideoX-5B but under the Adobe Research non-commercial licence. Neither can animate an existing delayered still layer today. (b) Matting: SAM 3 (custom SAM License, commercial use not restricted) plus MatAnyone 2 (NTU S-Lab non-commercial, human-only) or RVM (GPL-3.0, human-only) can cut a subject out of a finished clip for compositing, with alpha, on our own GPU.

## Findings

### A. Layered video decomposition (omnimatte)

- **Generative Omnimatte (Google DeepMind, U. Maryland, Weizmann; CVPR 2025 Highlight).** Input: a video plus binary object masks. Output: RGBA omnimatte layers, one per object with its shadows and reflections, plus a clean-plate background video. Stage 1 ("Casper", fine-tuned from Lumiere) removes each object's effects; stage 2 is test-time optimisation. Stated limits: captures colour-based effects (shadows, reflections) but not shape deformation; removal can fail with many objects; dynamic background effects (waves) can be mis-assigned. arXiv v1 2024-11-25, v2 2025-03-24. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16683 , https://gen-omnimatte.github.io/ (project page, undated beyond CVPR 2025).
- **Public reimplementation `gen-omnimatte-public`.** Apache-2.0 (the CogVideoX-5B base carries its own CogVideoX licence). Casper weights released for CogVideoX-Fun-V1.5-5b-InP, Wan2.1-Fun-1.3B-InP and Wan2.1-Fun-14B-InP (LoRA). The page says these "are close to, but do not match" the Lumiere-based Casper. Default resolution 384×672 (CogVideoX) or 480×832 (Wan). Runtime: Casper 1–2 min on an A100 or 4–5 min on a 48 GB A6000; omnimatte optimisation about 8 min on an A6000/A5000; the Gradio demo takes about 1 min removal plus 8 min optimisation per layer. Needs SAM2 installed. Repo page undated. https://github.com/gen-omnimatte/gen-omnimatte-public
- **OmnimatteZero (OriginAI, Hebrew U., Bar-Ilan, NVIDIA; SIGGRAPH Asia 2025).** Training-free, built on LTX-Video. Inputs per clip: `video.mp4`, `object_mask.mp4`, `total_mask.mp4` (object plus effects; can be derived from self-attention and refined with SAM2). Outputs: clean background, extracted foreground layer with effects, and recomposites by latent arithmetic; no alpha matte is produced, masks are binary. Project page claims 0.04 s/frame on an A100; the README says generation is slow and recommends fewer steps or lower resolution, default 512×768, "32GB+ VRAM recommended". **Licence: custom MIT-style text with "The Software may not be used for commercial purposes", including "using it in a paid service".** arXiv v1 2025-03-23, v3 2025-10-16. https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18033 , https://dvirsamuel.github.io/omnimattezero.github.io/ , https://github.com/dvirsamuel/OmnimatteZero , licence https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dvirsamuel/OmnimatteZero/main/LICENSE
- **EasyOmnimatte (Great Bay U., Adobe Research; CVPR 2026).** "Splits videos into layers (with effects) using 2-step diffusion without any post optimization." MIT badge. **Code and weights not released**: "The code is currently being organized and will be released upon acceptance"; inference code, checkpoints and training scripts are all open TODOs. arXiv 2512.21865. https://github.com/GVCLab/EasyOmnimatte (page undated)
- **LayerFlow (arXiv 2506.04228, submitted 2025-06-04, preprint).** A text-to-video DiT that jointly generates a transparent foreground, a clean background and the blended video, and can also decompose a blended video into layers or fill one layer given the other. Multi-stage LoRA training on layered images and copy-pasted video. Code release not confirmed on the arXiv page; how transparency is represented is not stated. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04228
- An optimisation-based comparison in the EasyOmnimatte line puts Generative Omnimatte at about 9 s per frame (search-digest figure, medium confidence). https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.21865

### B. Alpha-channel (RGBA) video generation

- **Wan-Alpha (arXiv 2509.24979).** Text-to-RGBA video on Wan2.1-T2V-14B with a VAE that maps alpha into the RGB latent, plus a LightX2V distillation LoRA. **Licence Apache-2.0.** v1.0 released 2025-09-30; v2.0 weights and inference code released 2025-12-16 (the card does not say what changed). Output: RGBA video rendered over a checkerboard plus PNG frames; separate preview and alpha videos in the showcase. Example: 832×480, 81 frames, 4 steps, run with `torchrun --nproc_per_node=8` under FSDP. **Image-to-video is not available**: "Release Wan-Alpha-I2V model weights" is an open to-do. Official ComfyUI version exists. https://huggingface.co/htdong/wan-alpha-v2.0 , https://huggingface.co/htdong/Wan-Alpha/blob/main/README.md
- **TransPixeler (formerly TransPixar; CVPR 2025).** Adds alpha-specific tokens and a LoRA to a pretrained video DiT to emit RGB plus alpha together. Released LoRA for CogVideoX-5B (49 frames, about 24 GB VRAM); a `wan` branch (2025-04-28) adds joint RGB-plus-mask training on Wan2.1 with no public weights. **Licence: "ADOBE RESEARCH LICENSE", "for noncommercial research purposes only"** (sections 1.1 and 1.3 exclude commercial products). Last dated news item 2025-04-28. https://github.com/wileewang/TransPixar , licence https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wileewang/TransPixar/main/LICENSE.md , paper https://arxiv.org/html/2501.03006v1

### C. Video matting and object tracking (cut a subject out of a finished clip)

- **SAM 3 (Meta; arXiv 2511.16719, v1 2025-11-20, v2 2026-03-28).** Detects, segments and tracks every instance of a concept given a noun phrase, an image exemplar, points, boxes or masks, across images and video; 848 M parameters; detector and tracker share one encoder; "SAM 3 doubles the accuracy of existing systems in both image and video PCS". SAM 3.1 "Object Multiplex" (2026-03-27) adds shared-memory joint multi-object tracking. Checkpoints are gated on Hugging Face. **Licence: "SAM License" (last updated 2025-11-19), a custom Meta agreement: royalty-free, derivative works are yours, no non-commercial clause; restrictions on prohibited end uses, reverse engineering, attribution in research, redistribution with the agreement, and a litigation-termination clause.** https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam3 , https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/sam3/main/LICENSE , https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16719
- **SAM 2 / 2.1 (Meta).** Apache-2.0. Promptable segmentation and tracking in video from clicks or boxes, propagating masklets; SAM 2.1 checkpoints 2024-09-29; 39.5–91.2 FPS on an A100 depending on size. Older than the brief's window but still the mask source every matting tool above assumes. https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam2
- **MatAnyone (CVPR 2025) and MatAnyone 2 (CVPR 2026 Highlight; NTU S-Lab, SenseTime).** Human video matting from a video plus a first-frame segmentation mask (the README points to the SAM2 demo for the mask); outputs a foreground video and an alpha video. MatAnyone 2 weights released 2026-03; training code, quality-evaluator checkpoint and dataset still open. **Licence: NTU S-Lab License 1.0, "Redistribution and use for non-commercial purpose"; section 4 says to contact the contributors for commercial use.** https://github.com/pq-yang/MatAnyone , https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pq-yang/MatAnyone/main/LICENSE , https://github.com/pq-yang/MatAnyone2
- **Robust Video Matting (ByteDance, WACV 2022).** Human-only, no trimap or mask needed, outputs alpha and foreground, 4K at about 74–76 FPS on a GTX 1080 Ti. **GPL-3.0** (re-released under GPL-3.0 on 2021-09-16). Old but still the only fully automatic option. https://github.com/PeterL1n/RobustVideoMatting
- **Matte Anything (HUST).** Image matting only (SAM + ViTMatte + GroundingDINO), MIT licence, outputs RGBA PNGs; last update 2024-05. Not a video tool. https://github.com/hustvl/Matte-Anything

## What this means for Napkin

- Keep delayering as a stills feature. There is no hosted API, and no commercially licensed open model that runs in seconds, that turns a generated clip into per-object layers with alpha. The best open option (gen-omnimatte-public, Apache-2.0) takes about 10 minutes per layer on a 48 GB GPU and needs masks; the fast one (OmnimatteZero) forbids paid-service use.
- Offer "edit a layer in the clip" as a re-render, not a transform. For a storyboard frame whose clip exists, the Runway routes are: `aleph2` with the edited still as a keyframe (the exact workflow Runway's Edit Studio sells: edit one frame, propagate), `klingO3_*` video_to_video with the new layer content as an image reference or element, and `product_swap` when the layer is a product. Budget $1.30–4.70 per 10 s attempt, and expect the model to change more than the one layer.
- Map layer vocabulary onto the edit types vendors actually support: swap product, replace subject, remove distraction, change background, relight. Do not promise "move" or "resize" for video; no API has a positional control, and Runway says its own recipe "struggles when the new product would not fit the action".
- Expect no alpha from any generated video. If the canvas composites a video layer over something else, the cut-out has to be made on our side with SAM 3 (SAM License allows it) and a matting model whose licence we can use: RVM is GPL-3.0 and human-only, MatAnyone/MatAnyone 2 are non-commercial without a separate agreement. For non-human objects we would be left with SAM 3 masks only (hard edges).
- If a "generate this layer as a transparent clip" feature is wanted later, Wan-Alpha (Apache-2.0) is the only permissive RGBA generator, but it is text-to-video only, 14B, and multi-GPU in the published example. Wait for Wan-Alpha-I2V before designing around it.
- Treat Pika as the one hosted API with an explicit mask or region field (`modify_region_mask`), useful as a comparison point, but it is outside our Runway-only provider decision and its catalog price ($0.03) conflicts with its own sample charge ($0.37).
- Note the churn: Gen-4 Aleph was sunset about a year after launch, and Google shut the Veo 3.1 preview on 2026-10-22 while Runway still lists it. Any video-edit route we build should sit behind the relay's provider abstraction and be versioned (`X-Runway-Version`, recipe `version`).

## Open questions

- The Runway request schema for `klingO3_*` on `/v1/video_to_video` (reference field names, element structure, tag syntax, maximum count) is not in either Runway SDK fetched today and the API reference is JavaScript-rendered. Someone with a key should pull the OpenAPI document or run one request.
- How faithfully does `aleph2` keep everything outside the edited layer when given a keyframe made by compositing a delayered still? Runway claims "changing only what you want to change" but publishes no numbers. A small test on our own frames, with a fixed seed, is the only way to know.
