# Interrogating the Client Brief


The client brief is a starting point, not a set of instructions. Clients brief the solution they have already decided on ("we need a radio campaign") rather than the problem ("the trade does not think of us first"). Your first job is to get behind the brief to the real problem, then reframe it in one sentence.


## The questions


**On the problem**
- What is actually going wrong in the business? What number is not where it should be?
- What is the client asking for, and what were they trying to fix by asking for it? Solve that.
- If communications did their job perfectly, what would change in the world? Is that change worth the money?
- What is the role for advertising here, honestly? Awareness, reframing, reminding, reassuring, recruiting? Advertising cannot fix a product or a price on its own; be clear what it can move.


**On the objective**
- What is the single most important thing to achieve, in the window that matters?
- How will anyone know it worked? Name the measure.
- What are we willing to not do, to do that one thing well?


**On the audience**
- Who exactly? Push past demographics to attitude and behaviour.
- What do they currently think, feel and do? What do we need them to think, feel and do instead?
- What is the barrier in their heads or habits? Indifference, a wrong belief, a habit, a fear, a better looking rival?
- Where and in what frame of mind will the message reach them?


**On the truth**
- What is genuinely true about the product or brand that earns the promise? The reasons to believe.
- What does the competition say, and what space is left that is both true for us and motivating?
- What are the real constraints: brand platform to sit under, tone, legal, media, budget, timings?


**On success and risk**
- What would make the client nervous? Naming the fear early saves the idea later.
- What is the one soft mandatory the client will not say but will kill work over (a brand line, a product hero, a founder's preference)? Treat stated brand preferences as soft mandatories.


## Reframe


Close the interrogation by writing the problem back in one plain sentence, from the audience's point of view, not the client's. Examples of the move:


- Client brief: "Launch our September van sale." Reframe: "The trade buys on habit and the incumbent owns the habit; give them a reason to reconsider in the months they actually order."
- Client brief: "Make people aware of our warranty." Reframe: "Buyers discount warranty as small print; make the long term promise feel like the reason to choose, not a footnote."


A sharp reframe is often the single most valuable thing a planner contributes. Everything downstream, the insight, the proposition, the brief, gets easier once the real problem is named.
