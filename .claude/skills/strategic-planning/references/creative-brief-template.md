# The Agency Brief (one page)


Short, plain, written to inspire. If it runs past one page it is a document, not a brief. Every line is written in language a normal person would say out loud.


## Template


- **Why are we here?** The business problem in one sentence, reframed from the audience's side.
- **What do we need to do?** The one objective, and the shift in what the audience thinks, feels or does.
- **Who are we talking to?** One person, by attitude and behaviour. Their from and to.
- **What do they currently believe?** The barrier the work has to move.
- **Insight.** The human truth we are building on.
- **Proposition.** The one thought. This is the springboard.
- **Why should they believe it?** Reasons to believe, kept short and honest.
- **Tone of voice.** How it should feel and sound.
- **Requirements.** Media, the brand line to sit under, mandatories, timings. Only what genuinely constrains the idea.


## Filled example (illustrative, trade van campaign)


- **Why are we here?** The trade buys vans on habit and the incumbent owns the habit; we need them to reconsider in the autumn months when they actually place orders.
- **What do we need to do?** Move dependable, design led van buyers from "I default to the usual" to "I would put my name on that one." Build the autumn order bank for the January plate change.
- **Who are we talking to?** A sole trader or small firm owner whose van is parked outside a customer's house before a word is spoken. Proud of the work, judged on turning up.
- **What do they currently believe?** "They are all much the same, and the one I know is safe."
- **Insight.** A tradesperson's van carries their name to every job, so the van letting them down and them letting a customer down are the same fear.
- **Proposition.** The van that works as hard for your reputation as you do.
- **Why should they believe it?** Long warranty, servicing and roadside cover; a dealer network that shows up; a brand people already trust on the drive.
- **Tone of voice.** Plain, warm, Irish, and quietly confident. Room for wit where it earns it. No jargon, no bluster.
- **Requirements.** Radio led, because they are in the van all day; outdoor and digital extend it. Sits under the master brand line. Whole range in view. Names the proof point exactly as the client brands it.


## Notes on writing it


- Write the proposition last and hardest. Everything above it should make it feel inevitable.
- Keep reasons to believe to the two or three that actually matter. A long list means you have not chosen.
- Do not bake the idea into the brief. If the brief already contains the ad, you have taken the fun and the surprise away from the people whose job it is.
- Read the whole thing aloud. If you stumble on a word or an idea, so will everyone else.
