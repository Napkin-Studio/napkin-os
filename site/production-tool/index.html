How it works

Three stages: Canvas, Storyboard, Video.

The tabs at the top of the tool walk you through them in order. You can go back to any stage at any time; what changed shows up as “behind” further along.

1

Canvas: make your characters

Draw, write or drop pictures anywhere. Then generate the characters and objects your ad needs, and give the keepers a name.

  • Draw or drop. Sketch on the canvas or drop in a picture. Write in a text box inside or beside your drawing: those words become the instruction.
  • Generate. Select any mix of drawings, pictures and notes, press Generate, type what you want, then Place… the result. It lands as a new picture you can use again.
  • Name it. Name a picture who_what, like @maya_front. Set as front, then Make the other views for three-quarter, side and back.
  • Use the names. @maya_laughing is that one picture; plain @maya is the whole character, its front plus up to three more pictures.
2

Storyboard: plan the shots, draw the frames

Write what happens, using your names. The tool splits it into shots and draws a frame for each, in order, so the place and the light carry through.

  • Script. Up to 600 characters, e.g. “@maya opens her umbrella and grins.” Pick 10, 15 or 20 s and 9:16, 1:1 or 16:9.
  • Plan shots. 2 to 8 shots, each with a framing, an action, a camera move and a length. Edit any of it, add or delete shots.
  • Draw frame 1, then Draw the rest. Frame 1 sets the place, light and style; each frame after follows the one before.
  • Change a frame. Type a change and Regenerate, or mark a Box and press Change the box. Older versions stay in the strip under the picture.
  • Update what follows. Change frame 1 and later frames show Behind frame 1. Press Remake them to redo just those, one by one, with your words for each.
3

Video: clips, notes, the finished ad

Lock the storyboard and each frame becomes a clip. Review it like a cut: pause, leave a note, make it again.

  • Make clips. One clip per shot, made from its frame.
  • Notes on the clip. Pause anywhere and type a note; it sticks to that moment. Make a new version sends your open notes as one change, and keeps the old take.
  • Fix it in the shot. Draw a Box with your note and the box is fixed in the shot’s frame first, then the clip is made again from it.
  • Pick your takes. v1, v2… choose which one the ad uses. New take makes the shot again, on any model you pick.
  • Render ad. Joins your chosen takes and ends on the 2.5 s “Made with Napkin Studio” card. Then Download.

Your crew

Three agents, each with one job.

They show up where the work is, so you can see who is doing what without reading a log.

Dex · directs

Plans, draws and makes.

Dex plans the shots, draws the frames and makes the clips. While a job runs, Dex is on its card: “Dex is drawing frame 2”.

On frame and shot cards, the job tray and the update box.

Ellis · reads

Reads your notes back.

Before anything is made again, Ellis reads your notes back in plain words, so you know what the next version will change.

At the head of the Notes panel on Video.

Jude · checks

Checks, and says when it needs you.

Jude checks each result as it lands. When a job fails or is waiting on the provider, Jude flags that card for you.

On failed cards and in the tray’s “Needs you” list.

The job tray in the top bar shows the crew’s faces and counts. Open it to see who is working and what needs you; Show jumps to the card.

Models

Runway does the work. The extras can’t break it.

Each step goes to an image or video model. You don’t have to choose: every first draw and first clip uses the step’s default.

Runway Main path · always there

Runway runs every step by default: characters, views, frames, box edits and clips. It needs nothing from you.

  • Frames and pictures: Gemini 3 Pro (or Gemini 3.1 Flash)
  • Clips: Veo 3.1 (or Veo 3.1 Fast); clip edits: Aleph 2

fal and HeyGen Optional extras

For the event you can bring your own fal or HeyGen key under Your keys. Their models then join the model menu as extras.

  • If an extra can’t take a job, Runway makes it instead, once, by itself. The card says “Made on Runway: fal could not”.
  • A refused prompt or a problem with your input doesn’t move to Runway: Runway would refuse it too.

Rolling out  Runway as the main path, with the extras falling back to it, is the agreed design and is being built now. Until it lands, which provider runs each step is set by the organisers.

Pick a model per version.

Once a frame or clip exists, the model chip beside Regenerate or New take opens the model menu. It lists each model with its provider, a short note and its price per job, and starts on the model that made the version you’re replacing.

When a job fails, Try another model sends the same request and the same references; only the model changes.

Veo works from the frame only and names characters in words; where exact characters matter, keep the default.

Tips and controls

Nothing you do is final.

Every version is kept, every change can be undone, and your work stays in your browser between visits.

Undo and redo

↶ and ↷ in the top bar undo anything you did to the project, any time, even after a reload. The button names the step (“Undo: deleted clip v3”). Results that landed since stay put. Jobs aren’t refunded by undo.

Ctrl+Z · Shift+Ctrl+Z

Save

Your project lives in this browser. Save sends a copy of the project to the server.

Ctrl+S

Export

From the ⋯ menu: a zip with your project (.clan, with its full history) and all its pictures and clips.

Box and brush edits

On a frame, hover the picture for its tools: Box (and Brush, where the model takes one). Mark the area, say what changes, press Change the box. The rest of the frame stays.

History

History in the top bar lists the changes to your project, and who made each one: you, Dex or a model.

FAQ and troubleshooting

When something doesn’t go to plan.

A step was refused. What do I change?

The card says what the limit was and what to change. There’s no Retry when sending the same thing again can’t work. Common ones:

  • A name with no picture (“Shot 2 uses @maya_jumping, but no image has that name”): name a picture that, or change the shot.
  • @maya has no front: select her best picture and press Set as front.
  • Content refused: the provider’s safety filter said no. Change the words or the pictures; it’s never retried by itself.
  • Too many characters in one shot: the tool keeps each character’s front and leaves out extra views, rather than refusing.

Anything unexpected: press Report on the card. It sends the error and the job ID to the organisers.

“No answer in time”: Stop waiting or Try again?

The provider didn’t confirm the job in time. It may still make it, or it may not, so the tool never sends it again on its own.

  • Stop waiting drops it. If the provider did take it, it may still be charged.
  • Try again sends it again. If the first one did go through, both may be charged.
What does “Behind frame 2” mean?

Something this frame or clip was made from has changed since, here frame 2. The amber chip names the cause; hover it for the reason. Nothing is redone without you.

When you’re ready, press Remake them on the bar at the bottom (“3 behind your changes”). The update box takes them one at a time: add words for that one, then Make it, or Do the rest as they are. Stop is in the top bar. Each new version replaces the old one only once it lands, and undo brings the old one back.

Where do my keys go?
  • Your fal or HeyGen key stays in this browser tab. Closing the tab forgets it, and Forget my keys clears it.
  • It’s never saved in your project or its Export.
  • It goes to the relay only when a job is sent. The relay keeps the key that job needs encrypted, and never logs it or sends it back.
A job says “Checking…” or sits in the queue.

In the queue means it’s waiting for a free slot (your two at once, or the provider’s). It starts by itself. Checking is Jude making sure the provider has it; the tool never sends a job twice, so you’re never charged twice for one.

Where is my work kept?

In this browser, as you go, so a reload brings everything back, including jobs still running. Save (Ctrl+S) sends a copy to the server, and Export downloads the whole project as a zip.
